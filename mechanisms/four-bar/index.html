Key Terms & Formulas: RPM, Torque, Load & Signals

RPM & Angular Velocity\omega = \frac{2\pi \cdot \text{RPM}}{60}

Revolutions Per Minute measures shaft speed. Converted to angular velocity $\omega$ (rad/s) for mechanical work. Power is $P = \tau \cdot \omega$.

Torque & Stall Torque\tau = F \times r \text{ [kg}\cdot\text{cm / N}\cdot\text{m]}

Rotational force. Stall torque is maximum output at 0 RPM; rated torque is safe continuous limit before thermal overheating. $1\text{ N}\cdot\text{m} \approx 10.2\text{ kg}\cdot\text{cm}$.

Mechanical Load\tau_{\text{load}} \text{ vs. Speed Curve}

Resistance torque on the shaft. Higher load slows the rotor, decreasing back-EMF so armature current rises automatically to match load torque.

Back-EMF (Generator Effect)V_{\text{bemf}} = k_e \cdot \omega

Coils spinning through the stator magnetic field induce reverse voltage opposing supply voltage. Net driving voltage is $V_{\text{net}} = V_{\text{avg}} - V_{\text{bemf}}$.

Gear Ratio & Efficiency\tau_{\text{out}} = \eta \cdot G \cdot \tau_{\text{motor}}

A gearhead divides speed $\omega_{\text{out}} = \omega_m / G$ while multiplying torque by $\eta \cdot G$, where $\eta \approx 90\text{–}95\%$ per spur gear stage.

Current & Thermal DissipationI = \frac{V - V_{\text{bemf}}}{R}, P = I^2 R

At stall ($\omega = 0$), $V_{\text{bemf}} = 0$ so current peaks at $I_{\text{stall}} = V/R_{\text{coil}}$, causing rapid thermal rise. Never stall under full voltage.

Control: PWM vs. Pulse Width\text{PWM \% vs. 1000–2000 }\mu\text{s}

DC & gearmotors use high-frequency PWM duty cycle to vary average voltage/speed; hobby servos read absolute 50 Hz pulse duration to command shaft position.

Encoder Resolution (CPR)\text{CPR} = 4 \times \text{slots} \times G

Quadrature encoders count all rising and falling edges of channels A & B ($4\times$ decoding), giving sub-degree precision per output turn.
