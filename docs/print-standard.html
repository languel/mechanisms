FDM Mechanical Engineering Reference · Illustrated Vocabulary

The Illustrated Print Standard

Every mechanism and motor demonstrator in this catalogue is built from one shared vocabulary of dimensions, fits, shaft bores, and mesh verification rules — so a post from one kit fits a cap from another, and every term below can be seen directly in the parts you print.

1. The Vertical Z-Stack Vocabulary

All parts are authored in millimetres with z = 0 as the flat printer build plate and +z pointing up. In the assembled mechanism, rotating and sliding parts stack along +z in standardized 8.0 mm turning layers.

Base plate (Frame)

4.0 mm · 20 layers

What it is: The stationary ground link (frame) that holds axle posts, linear guide rails, and travel scales at exact centre distances. At 0.20 mm layer height, 4.0 mm equals 20 solid layers — rigid across a 200 mm span without warping.

Body thickness (Gears, Cams & Links)

6.0 mm · 3 walls

What it is: Every moving gear, cam, pawl, and linkage bar has a 6.0 mm structural face thickness. Sliced with 3 perimeters on a 0.4 mm nozzle (1.2 mm walls) and 25% infill, teeth and pin bosses resist shear loads cleanly.

Stand-off hub & Turning layer pitch

1.6 mm hub · 8.0 mm pitch

What it is: Every rotating part has a 1.6 mm raised boss (hub) on its underside around the bore. When placed on the baseplate, only the narrow hub touches the plate, lifting the wide gear or link face by 1.6 mm so it spins with low friction and zero bought washers.

Pivot post & Snap-fit Press cap

Ø6.8 mm post · Ø13.2 mm cap

What it is: Instead of requiring metal bolts for every joint, baseplates and cranks carry integral Ø6.8 mm cylindrical posts. A 3.0 mm thick press cap with a Ø6.5 mm inner bore pushes snugly onto the tip of the post to lock the assembly axially.

2. Fits, Clearances & Motor Shaft Bores

FDM 3D printers slightly shrink internal holes because molten filament pulls inward around curves. Each mechanism exposes a live Bore clearance slider (default 0.30 mm) and a Motor shaft selector that cuts the exact bore geometry directly into the exported STL.

Running fit vs. Interference press fit

+0.30 mm turn · −0.30 mm press

What it is: A running fit enlarges the bore by +0.30 mm (Ø7.10 mm on a Ø6.80 mm post) so gears spin freely right off the bed. A press fit undersizes the bore by −0.30 mm so retainer caps grip the post by elastic hoop tension.

Linear guide & slot clearance

0.35 mm per side

What it is: Wherever a linear rack, piston slider, cam follower stem, or Scotch yoke glides between parallel guide rails, a 0.35 mm gap per side prevents elephant-foot or corner bulge from binding the stroke.

Motor D-shafts & Servo spline bores

NEMA 17 · N20 · SG90 · MG996R

What it is: Round motor shafts slip under torque. Selecting NEMA 17 (5 mm D-shaft) or N20 (3 mm D-shaft) on any gear or crank page cuts an internal flat face that locks rotation positively; selecting SG90 or MG996R sizes the bore for hobby servo splines.

3. Gear Tooth Geometry & Mesh Verification Rules

Every STL exported by this workbench is generated from exact 2D mathematical profiles and verified by an automated topological mesh audit before download.

Module (m = d / z) & 20° Pressure Angle

m = 1.5–3.0 mm · α = 20°

What it is: The module m sets tooth size: pitch diameter is d = m · z and centre distance is C = m(z₁ + z₂)/2. All gears use a standard 20° pressure angle with 0.20 mm backlash thinning so printed teeth roll without binding.

Watertight manifold & Outward winding

Euler e₊ = e₋ · V > 0

What it is: Slicers reject STLs with holes, flipped triangles, or self-intersecting bosses. Before export, every part is float32-quantized to 0.1 µm and checked that every directed edge matches its reverse, signed volume is positive, and stacked slabs share flush z boundaries without overlapping.

Zero supports & Flat bed orientation

0 supports · ≤ 12 mm bridges

What it is: Every part in the .zip pack and combined bed .stl is pre-oriented with its largest flat face at z = 0. Even multi-tier parts print upwards from a flat base without support material to pry out of tooth roots or bores.

Tune once, print everywhere: If your classroom printer extrudes slightly wide, raise the Bore clearance slider on any mechanism page from 0.30 mm to 0.40 mm and click All Parts (.stl) or Print pack (.zip) — every bore, slot, and rail gap updates in real time.
